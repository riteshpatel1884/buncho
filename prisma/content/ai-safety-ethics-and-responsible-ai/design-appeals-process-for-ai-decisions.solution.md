**Principles:** contestability, timeliness, **meaningful human review**, transparency, no retaliation, accessibility.

**Process flow**

1. **Notice at decision time:** state that AI was used, the decision, main reasons (reason codes), data used, and how to appeal (button/link/phone), deadline, and what evidence helps.
2. **Easy intake:** in-product form, multiple channels, accessible and multilingual, free of charge, reference number, ability to attach evidence and correct inaccurate data.
3. **Triage by severity:** urgent or high-impact cases (healthcare, livelihoods) fast-tracked; SLAs (e.g. acknowledge in 24h, decide within 7 to 30 days).
4. **Independent human review:** trained reviewers with authority and context (decision log, explanation, original data), not simply confirming the AI score; guard against **automation bias** (hide the AI recommendation initially for some cases, require written rationale); escalation to senior/ombuds panel.
5. **Outcome and remedy:** uphold, reverse, correct data, compensate; give a written explanation and next steps (external appeal, regulator, arbitration).
6. **Re-scoring** with corrected data and **record** everything in the audit trail.

**Learning loop**

- Track metrics: appeal rate, **overturn rate** (high = model/policy problem), time to resolution, disparities by group in appeals and overturns, user satisfaction.
- Feed overturned cases into error analysis, retraining/threshold review, fairness audits and test sets.
- Trigger incident review if overturn rates spike.

**Compliance:** GDPR Art. 22(3), EU AI Act rights to explanation and human oversight, ECOA adverse-action rules, platform regulations like the DSA's internal complaint-handling systems.
