**Interpretability:** the degree to which a human can understand the **model's internal mechanism** (how inputs map to outputs) *by design*: linear models, shallow decision trees, rule lists, GAMs, sparse models. **Intrinsic** and global.

**Explainability (XAI):** producing human-understandable reasons for a model's behaviour, often **post hoc** for opaque models: why *this* prediction? Local or global.

| | Interpretability | Explainability |
|---|---|---|
| Question | How does the model work? | Why did it output this? |
| When | Built in | Added after training (or generated by the model) |
| Fidelity | Faithful by construction | May be an approximation and can mislead |
| Examples | Linear weights, tree paths, GAM shapes | SHAP, LIME, counterfactuals, saliency/Grad-CAM, attention views, example-based, chain-of-thought |

**Methods:** feature attribution (SHAP, Integrated Gradients), surrogate models (LIME), counterfactual explanations ("approved if income were +5k"), partial dependence, concept-based (TCAV), mechanistic interpretability for LLMs.

**Why it matters**

- **Trust and adoption**, debugging and finding spurious features/leakage.
- **Regulation:** GDPR Art. 22 safeguards, EU AI Act transparency, ECOA adverse-action notices, model risk management.
- **Fairness audits**, safety and accountability, scientific insight.

**Cautions:** explanations must be **faithful, stable and actionable**; LLM chain-of-thought is not guaranteed to reflect true computation; attention is not explanation; test explanations with users. In high-stakes domains prefer **inherently interpretable models** when accuracy is similar.
