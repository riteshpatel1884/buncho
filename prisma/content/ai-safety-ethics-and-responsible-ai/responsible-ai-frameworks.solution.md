**Responsible AI:** developing and deploying AI so that it is **safe, fair, transparent, accountable, privacy-preserving, and beneficial**, with risks identified and managed through the lifecycle.

**Common principles:** fairness and non-discrimination; reliability and safety; privacy and security; transparency/explainability; accountability and human oversight; inclusiveness; societal and environmental well-being.

**Frameworks and standards**

| Framework | Nature |
|---|---|
| **NIST AI RMF 1.0** (Govern, Map, Measure, Manage) + Generative AI Profile | Voluntary risk framework (US) |
| **EU AI Act** | Binding risk-tiered law |
| **ISO/IEC 42001** | Certifiable AI management system |
| **ISO/IEC 23894** | AI risk management guidance |
| **OECD AI Principles / UNESCO Recommendation** | International principles |
| **Company frameworks** (Microsoft Responsible AI Standard, Google AI Principles, Anthropic RSP-style policies) | Internal governance |
| **IEEE 7000 series**, sector rules (SR 11-7 for banks, FDA SaMD guidance, HIPAA) | Specific domains |

**Implementation**

1. **Governance:** AI policy, risk appetite, ethics/review board, clear owners, approval gates.
2. **Inventory and risk-tier** every AI system; run **impact assessments** (DPIA/AIA).
3. **Controls across the lifecycle:** data governance, bias and robustness testing, red teaming, privacy engineering, human oversight, guardrails.
4. **Documentation:** model cards, datasheets, decision logs, audit trails.
5. **Monitoring and incident response**, user recourse, third-party/vendor reviews.
6. **Training and culture**, metrics and regular audits.

Turn principles into **testable requirements with thresholds** or they stay slogans.
