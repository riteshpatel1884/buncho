The **NIST AI RMF 1.0 (Jan 2023)** is a **voluntary**, sector-agnostic US framework to manage AI risks to individuals, organisations and society. A **Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1, 2024)** adds GenAI-specific risks.

**Trustworthy AI characteristics:** valid and reliable; safe; secure and resilient; accountable and transparent; explainable and interpretable; privacy-enhanced; fair with harmful bias managed.

**Four core functions**

| Function | Purpose | Example activities |
|---|---|---|
| **GOVERN** (cross-cutting) | Culture, policies, accountability, risk tolerance | AI policy, roles, third-party risk, legal compliance, staff training |
| **MAP** | Understand context and identify risks | Intended use, stakeholders, impacts, data and model limits, risk categorisation |
| **MEASURE** | Analyse, assess and track risks | Metrics, TEVV (test, evaluation, verification, validation), bias, robustness, red teaming, monitoring |
| **MANAGE** | Prioritise and treat risks | Mitigate/transfer/accept, incident response, decommissioning, continuous improvement |

**GenAI risks highlighted:** confabulation, harmful content, privacy and data leakage, IP, information integrity (deepfakes), CBRN information, cybersecurity, value-chain and component integration, environmental impact, human-AI configuration.

**Using it**

1. Create a **profile** (target vs current state) for each system.
2. Map existing controls to the functions, find gaps.
3. Embed measures into the lifecycle (CI evals, release gates, monitoring).
4. Document decisions and residual risk.
5. It is **not certification**; combine with ISO/IEC 42001 and regulation (EU AI Act) as needed.

**vs EU AI Act:** NIST is guidance and process-oriented; the EU Act is binding law with risk tiers.
