**What the interviewer is really testing**

Tests your skepticism and validation habits, making sure you don't blindly accept AI "hallucinations".

**Expert answer**

> Yes, absolutely. I was using a generative AI tool to help summarize a long, technical industry regulation report. While checking its summary, I noticed it stated a specific legal compliance deadline that seemed incorrect based on my previous knowledge. Instead of copying it over, I manually cross-referenced the original government document and realized the AI had completely hallucinated the date. I corrected the data in my final brief and learned never to accept AI outputs without human verification. I use AI to accelerate my work, but I retain full accountability for the accuracy of the final product.

**Why this works**

It gives a concrete example, shows the verification step against a primary source, states the correction and ends with ownership of the final output.

**Tips to make it yours**

- Say "yes" with a real example. "AI never makes mistakes for me" is a red flag.
- Show the verification step: what you checked against, and what you changed.
