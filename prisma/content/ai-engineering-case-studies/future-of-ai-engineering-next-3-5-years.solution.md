**Frame it as trends plus implications, with humility about uncertainty.**

**Likely trends**

1. **Agents and automation of multi-step work:** more reliable tool use, computer use, long-running workflows; the engineering challenge moves to **reliability, permissions, evaluation and oversight** of autonomous systems.
2. **Evaluation and observability become core disciplines:** eval-driven development, continuous production evaluation, tracing, and AI-specific testing are standard parts of CI/CD.
3. **Models get cheaper, faster and more capable; smaller specialised models** (distillation, on-device, MoE) handle many tasks; **routing** across models is normal, so cost/latency/quality optimisation is a daily skill.
4. **Multimodal and real-time interfaces:** voice, vision, video, and screens as inputs; low-latency speech-to-speech.
5. **Context, memory and retrieval:** longer context but still need retrieval, personalisation, memory management and data permissions; "context engineering" over prompt tinkering.
6. **Standards and infrastructure:** protocols (MCP, A2A), AI gateways, managed agent runtimes, better tooling for guardrails and governance.
7. **Regulation and responsible AI:** EU AI Act obligations, auditability, privacy, safety evaluations become engineering requirements.
8. **AI-assisted software engineering:** coding agents change how we build; engineers spend more time on specification, review, architecture and verification.

**What probably stays valuable:** software engineering fundamentals, system design, data and evaluation rigour, domain knowledge, product judgement, security thinking, and the ability to communicate trade-offs. **Commodity risk:** thin wrappers and prompt-only differentiation.

**My positioning:** "I focus on durable skills (evals, retrieval, system reliability, cost) and keep experimenting with new tools so I can adopt what proves itself."

Acknowledge uncertainty ("it may move faster or slower than expected") and give a falsifiable view.
