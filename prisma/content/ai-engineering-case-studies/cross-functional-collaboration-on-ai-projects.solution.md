**What interviewers look for:** you translate between disciplines, align on goals, handle disagreement, and deliver together.

**Who you work with and what you give/get**

| Partner | What you do together |
|---|---|
| **Product/business** | Define the problem, success metrics, scope, ROI; manage expectations about uncertainty |
| **Design/UX** | Interaction patterns for probabilistic output: citations, confidence, feedback buttons, fallbacks, error states |
| **Data/analytics** | Data sources, quality, labelling, dashboards, A/B tests |
| **Software/platform/DevOps** | APIs, latency, scaling, CI/CD, observability, cost |
| **Domain experts** | Ground truth, rubrics, review of outputs, edge cases |
| **Legal/security/compliance** | Privacy, IP, risk assessment, guardrails, vendor review |
| **Support/operations** | Failure reports, feedback loops, playbooks |

**Practices that make it work**

- **Shared language:** explain concepts with examples, avoid jargon, write short design docs with eval metrics and risks.
- **Shared eval set** built with domain experts, so everyone argues about the same examples rather than opinions.
- **Regular demos on real inputs;** a living "known issues" list; clear decision owners (RACI).
- **Early involvement** of legal/security; design reviews before building.
- **Resolve conflict with data:** e.g. product wants a faster launch, safety wants more guardrails → propose a limited rollout with metrics and a rollback plan.

**Answer template (STAR):** "On [project] I partnered with [functions]. The challenge was [misalignment, e.g. support wanted fewer escalations while compliance wanted stricter refusals]. I [facilitated an eval-set workshop, defined a combined metric, ran an A/B]. Result: [metric], and the team adopted [process]. I learned [lesson]." (Fill with your real story; if you have limited professional experience, use university, internship, open-source or hackathon team projects and say so honestly.)

**Avoid:** blaming other teams, claiming sole credit, or vague "we collaborated well" without specifics.
