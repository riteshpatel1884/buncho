**Model answer**

No career is guaranteed, but GenAI is a strong bet **if you build skills that outlast any single tool**.

**Why demand is real**

- Adoption is spreading across healthcare, finance, customer support, software development and automation, and companies need people who can ship reliable systems, not just demos.
- Moving from a prototype to something safe, fast, cheap and evaluated is hard, and that engineering gap creates durable work.

**What changes quickly (do not anchor on it)**

- Specific models, frameworks and prompt tricks go out of date within months.
- Some routine tasks (boilerplate code, simple content) become automated, so the role shifts toward design, evaluation and judgement.

**Durable skills to invest in**

- Fundamentals: Python, statistics, ML basics, how Transformers work.
- Data and retrieval: RAG, embeddings, vector search, data quality.
- Evaluation and observability: test sets, metrics, monitoring, cost and latency.
- Responsible AI: privacy, bias, safety, guardrails.
- Software engineering: APIs, testing, deployment, cloud.
- Domain knowledge, which makes the AI useful in a specific industry.

**Closing line:** "I treat it as a field where continuous learning is part of the job: I focus on fundamentals and production skills, and I stay current by building small projects with new tools."
